Data protection

Last updated {{ brand.legal_updated }}

This page is for the person who has to sign off on using {{ brand.name }}: a clinic's data protection officer, a law firm's partner, an agency owner who would rather know than hope. It describes what the system does now. Where something is not finished, it says so instead of describing the plan.

Not yet reviewed by a lawyer. This is an honest engineering description of where data goes and what happens to it. The transfer mechanisms below are marked unfinished because stating one we have not had reviewed would be worse than stating none.

Who is the controller, and who is the processor

This is the distinction everything else follows from, and it has two different answers depending on whose data you mean.

  • Your customers' and patients' data is yours. You are the controller. We are your processor, and we process it only to run your front desk - answering messages, taking bookings, telling you what needs you. We do not use it for anything else, we do not sell it, and it is not used to train any model.
  • Your own data is ours to control. Your name, your business's contact details, what you asked us for. We are the controller for that, with you as the data subject.

The agreement that puts the first of those in writing is our data processing agreement, which is Art. 28 and which we sign with every customer.

What we process, and why

  • Messages. What your customer wrote and what the assistant replied, in full. Without it the assistant cannot hold a conversation, and you cannot see what was said in your name.
  • Phone numbers and names. The address a reply goes to, and what to call the person.
  • The language they wrote in. So the next reply is in the same one.
  • Appointments. Time, service and who it is for.
  • Call audio and transcripts, for businesses that have the voice channel switched on.

Our lawful basis as controller for your own data is contract - Art. 6(1)(b) - except for the signup form, where it is your consent under Art. 6(1)(a) and you can withdraw it by telling us to. For your customers' data, the lawful basis is yours to determine, which is what being the controller means.

Clinics, and health data

The assistant is built not to collect clinical information. It will not ask about symptoms, medication, medical history or whether someone is suitable for a treatment; it will not discuss them if a patient volunteers them; and it will never ask for a photograph. A message that turns clinical is handed to a human and the assistant stops replying.

This is not a setting someone has to remember to switch on. It is part of what the assistant is permitted to say, it is the same for every clinic, and an edit in our own console cannot remove it. Tests fail the build if it goes missing.

What follows from that: the treatment a patient enquired about is stored, because it is what they wrote and what the appointment is for. Anything beyond it is not solicited. We are not asking you to trust that nobody wrote it down - we are telling you the assistant is not able to ask.

Art. 9 still applies to what a patient volunteers in their own words, and our agreement with you covers it. If your own assessment is that an enquiry about a dental treatment is special-category data in your hands, that assessment is the one that governs.

How long we keep it

Today, indefinitely. There is no automatic deletion. A conversation from the day a business started is still in the database. Expired login sessions are the only thing anything removes on its own.

That is not where this should end up, and we are not going to print a retention period we do not enforce. When deletion runs on a schedule, this paragraph will say what the schedule is.

What we will do now, on request: delete a business's data, or one person's data, by hand, within 30 days. Ask at {{ brand.dpo_email }}. Deleting a business deletes everything belonging to it, because the database is built that way - conversations, messages, customers, bookings and call records all go with it.

Your rights, and your customers'

Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection - Arts. 15 to 21. If you are one of our customers' customers, the business you messaged is the controller and the request is theirs to answer; we help them answer it. If you are our customer, write to us directly.

  • Data protection contact: {{ brand.dpo_email }}
  • Controller: {{ brand.legal_name }}, {{ brand.address }}
  • Supervisory authority: {{ brand.supervisory_authority }}
  • Representative in the EU (Art. 27): {{ brand.eu_representative }}

You can complain to your supervisory authority at any time, and you do not have to come to us first.

Automated replies, and the EU AI Act

The assistant writes its own replies. It tells your customers that it is an assistant and not a person, in the first message it sends them, in their own language - Art. 50 of the AI Act, and also simply the decent thing to do. It never claims to be a human being, and if anyone asks it directly, it says so.

It does not make decisions about people. It books appointments, answers questions from material you gave it, and hands the conversation to you when it should not be the one answering. There is no profiling and no automated decision-making in the Art. 22 sense.

Security

  • Encrypted in transit, everywhere.
  • Encrypted at rest by the database provider.
  • One business can never see another's data. Every query is scoped to the business it belongs to, and there is a test suite whose whole job is to try to break that.
  • Business owners sign in with a one-time code sent to their own phone. There are no shared passwords.
  • Access to the production database is limited to the people who run the service.

Breaches

If something happens to your data we tell you without undue delay, and in any case within 72 hours of finding out, with what we know, what we are doing about it and what you have to do - Art. 33(2), which puts that duty on us as your processor.

Changes

When this page changes in a way that matters, we tell our customers before it takes effect rather than after. The date at the top is the version.
